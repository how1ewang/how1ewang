## Case study

### YuanQiPai — integrated commerce platform &nbsp;<sub>2025 – present · product owner & sole builder</sub>

A biotech company sells health products through a dealer network and directly to consumers on WeChat. It ran on disconnected tools: no single view of members, dealers, orders or commissions, and every marketing change needed outside help.

- **Discovery & scope** — mapped how members, dealers, orders and money actually moved, then shaped that into 17 product areas with a phased plan: storefront first, loyalty and dealer tools next, content and training after.
- **What was built** — a WeChat Mini Program storefront (58 screens), a staff console (42 screens), the platform behind them, and a stand-alone customer registration tool; 11 partners integrated for payments, ERP, cloud and the WeChat ecosystem.
- **Launch** — WeChat platform compliance, entity-change filing, food-seller registration paperwork, domain and HTTPS, a launch-readiness checklist.
- **Operate & iterate** — structured feedback rounds turned into 50 releases in six months; an illustrated operations guide so staff run day-to-day changes without a developer.
- **Decisions that mattered** — a drag-and-drop page builder so marketing never waits for a developer; commission rules as console settings rather than code; a zero-downtime data migration because dealers work in the daytime; automatic refunds for payments that arrive after cancellation.

The source is private (client code). The full case study, with the decisions and their outcomes, is on [the site →](https://how1ewang.github.io/#work)

### Customer Registration System &nbsp;<sub>2026 · product owner & sole builder</sub>

Sales staff in 20 countries were collecting customer details on paper. In ten weeks that became a phone form anyone can hand to a customer (3,423-district address picker, ID photo capture, feedback survey), a map dashboard the owner can check at any time, and a feed that keeps the main platform consistent without duplicate records.
